With <FaviconLink href="https://numa.channel/how-it-sounds" external>numa</FaviconLink>, you can write a thought and hear it turned into twelve seconds of music. Each one becomes a small orb in a public gallery, where other people can listen.

The idea grew out of a <FaviconLink href="https://www.youtube.com/@numa.000" external>YouTube channel I started</FaviconLink> for minimalist lo-fi sound journals. Each numbered release brings together ten tracks and a continuous mix around a feeling or a moment. Later, I wanted to let other people bring their own thoughts into it. That became How It Sounds.

## A thought, then a sound

The page asks one question: “What is on your mind?”

You have up to 240 characters to answer. It could be a memory, something bothering you, or a thought you haven't quite finished.

<FaviconLink href="https://elevenlabs.io/blog/eleven-music-now-available-in-the-api#get-started-today" external>ElevenLabs Music</FaviconLink> generates an instrumental from what you write. The direction I give the model is to interpret the feeling behind the words. A thought about an empty room might carry loneliness, relief, or the discomfort of leaving somewhere familiar. The result should respond to that feeling, rather than just make a sound associated with a room.

There is room for interpretation here. I don't expect a model to know exactly what someone meant. Part of the experience is hearing what comes back and deciding how it relates to what you wrote.

## Why a gallery

The memory orbs in *Inside Out* were another inspiration. I liked the idea of a memory becoming something you could see, and of many of those memories forming a collection. That influenced the gallery in How It Sounds: each orb holds someone's thought and a short piece of music you can play.

You can play a sound directly from its orb, or open its page to share it or download the audio.

<NumaSoundGallery client:visible />

That shared space is what interested me about extending the channel. Someone leaves a sentence, and another person can come across it later and hear its interpretation. The collection grows from what people choose to share.

There are no accounts or private entries. The page tells you before submitting that your thought and its sound will be public. That matters for a prompt that invites people to write something personal.
